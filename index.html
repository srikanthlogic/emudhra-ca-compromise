eMudhra CA compromise · disclosed 20 October 2026 · CashlessConsumer consumer guide

Eight “secret” keys anyone could guess. One was the number 4.

India's largest private certificate authority issued eight intermediates whose private keys carry 3–8 bits of randomness. Browsers worldwide distrusted them within days. No Indian authority has said a word.

The whole story in five facts

  1. What a certificate authority is. Browsers trust a short list of companies to vouch for websites. eMudhra Ltd, a listed Bengaluru company (NSE: EMUDHRA), is one of them, under its emSign brand, and also issues the digital signature certificates used for income-tax and MCA filings.[1][2]
  2. What broke. Between them, the eight compromised intermediates could mint browser-trusted certificates for any website: your bank, your email, UIDAI. The private keys protecting them had 3 to 8 bits of randomness. A real key has 2384 possibilities. These had between 8 and 256. One was literally 4, and an AI agent recovered a key on its first attempt.[3] [4]
  3. How it surfaced. A researcher known as “Wayne” reported it on 17 October, proving control by signing a taunt with each key. Firefox and Chrome distrusted all eight within about a day. Apple followed on 19 October. The public test site went up on 20 October.[5]
  4. No evidence of interception. Certificate Transparency logs show no malicious certificates were ever issued under the eight.[3] The failure was caught before it was used, as far as anyone can currently show.
  5. The company's own account does not comfort. Keys meant to stay inside tamper-proof hardware were shipped to US and EU data centres under an “approved deviation” for “data sovereignty”, and a script flaw let test keys sign production certificates. A re-revocation failed verification, so the company re-signed it with the same broken keys rather than halt issuance.[3]

This page is our consumer guide. The formal record is in our statement and our open letter to the Reserve Bank of India and MeitY.

What this means for you

The padlock in your browser stands for a promise: this really is your bank. The promise is kept by certificate authorities. For a window of days, anyone holding these keys and a network position between you and any website could impersonate that website so well that your browser would congratulate them for it.

What protected you

  • An updated Chrome, Firefox, or Safari. All three distrusted the eight intermediates within days.
  • Apps that pin their own connections. Signal, WhatsApp, WireGuard. They don't depend on your system's trust store.

What did not

  • Command-line and packaging tools. curl, pip, package managers, CI systems, anything bundling its own CA store. If your tooling still trusts the old roots, the interception math still works.
  • Old, unupdated devices. Smart TVs, routers, old Android phones, forgotten servers. They update rarely or never.
  • Reportedly Edge on Windows 10 in some configurations, per incident discussion.

Do this now, in five minutes

  1. Update your browser, phone and laptop. Current Chrome and Firefox carry the fix. That one step covers most of the risk.
  2. Check your own machines: 
    curl https://dv-g3b-emudhra.we-have-a-problem.com/
curl https://dv-g3b-aaa-emudhra.we-have-a-problem.com/
    A certificate error means your tooling is safe. If the page loads, you still trust broken keys; update.
  3. Run a server or CI? Inventory everything that verifies certificates, and pin patched trust stores: certifi 2026.10.19, Go 1.27.3, rustls-webpki 0.103.16 or later.

The India exposure, checked by us

  • UIDAI's own website serves its certificate from eMudhra. So does GeM, the government marketplace. These are different, valid chains, not the compromised ones. But Chrome is now formally reviewing eMudhra's remaining root, which puts both in scope for future distrust. Who is this company? We traced its full corporate history.
  • IDRBT, the RBI-established institute that runs the banking sector's certifying authority, builds its issuance on eMudhra's emCA platform.[6][7]
  • eMudhra Consumer Services sells the digital signature certificates millions use for income-tax and MCA filings. The CCA-licensed hierarchy it runs was not part of this compromise.
  • The devices least likely to ever be fixed are everywhere here. An old Android phone that never sees a trust-store update is exposed until it is replaced.

The structural point survives every caveat: the trust mark on India's digital economy failed a basic competence test in public, and the supervisory apparatus has said nothing.

What we are demanding

Full detail in the open letter. In short:

  1. CCA India: a public, verifiable investigation with published findings, and supervision that does not depend on foreign root programs to surface failures by Indian licensees.
  2. Government buyers (MeitY, GeM, UIDAI): publish your CA dependencies and your incident response. You bought trust services; consumers are owed the assurance you paid for.
  3. IDRBT / .bank.in: disclose the dependency of the banking PKI on vendor platforms like eMudhra's emCA, and the controls that apply.
  4. RBI: issue supervisory guidance on trust-store hygiene for regulated entities' apps and channels, and say publicly whether customer-facing banking traffic was ever at risk.
